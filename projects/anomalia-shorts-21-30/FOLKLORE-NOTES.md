# Editorial folklore notes — FILE 021–030

This batch uses names and broad locations associated with stories circulating in Indonesia. It is designed as entertainment, not ethnography or proof of supernatural events.

- **Orang Pendek — Kerinci, Jambi:** adapted as a camera-trap cryptid mystery.
- **Begu Ganjang — Sumatra Utara:** adapted around an unnaturally elongating shadow; no ritual practice is depicted.
- **Palasik — Sumatra Barat:** adapted as an ambiguous airborne thermal anomaly; graphic or child-targeting elements are excluded.
- **Lampor — Yogyakarta/Javanese circulation:** adapted as an unseen night procession and impossible road record.
- **Ahool — Gunung Salak, Jawa Barat:** adapted as a giant nocturnal winged cryptid.
- **Saranjana — Kotabaru, Kalimantan Selatan:** adapted as an invisible city visible through a camera.
- **Kuyang — Kalimantan:** adapted as a non-graphic floating thermal silhouette.
- **Leak — Bali:** adapted as a fictional shape-changing light and shadow; no sacred ceremony or ritual instruction is depicted.
- **Ebu Gogo — Flores, NTT:** adapted as small cave-dwelling figures that mimic speech.
- **Parakang — Sulawesi Selatan:** adapted as a shapeshifter whose animal appearance has human body heat.

All protagonists, recordings, timestamps, and plot events are original fictional constructs. On-screen labeling: `FIKSI • ANALOG HORROR`.
